## TOP — Nate Herk: why the giant frame works
- **Hook (0:00–0:37):** capability MONTAGE on his own video ("text on screen like up
  here… subtitles down at the bottom… I can move my face around") — **the video
  demonstrates itself** while he talks. Payoff promise 0:24, then the giveaway promise
  0:31 ("repo + skill + everything, completely free"). Then quantifies the pain

- **Structure:** long (32 min), hands-on, exact prompts read aloud to paste; heavy

- **Steal:** self-demonstrating hook · quantify the manual-editing pain · give the
  starter away free · show your iterations (authenticity + Gate-2 material variation).

## BREAKOUT — Simon Crowe (the one to emulate; 19× views/sub)
- **Hook (0:00–0:25):** identity twist — "This is a complete click-by-click tutorial.
  There's just one problem. What you're watching right now isn't me." Pattern interrupt
  + product demo IN the hook. Then a time-boxed ROADMAP ("in the next 18 minutes:
  build → take control → clone you → faceless characters") and a competence promise.
- **Why a 7.4k channel got 140k: SEARCH.** Title = the exact beginner query ("How to
  Use HeyGen") on a high-search BRAND term, delivered genuinely click-by-click
  (signup at 0:57, every toggle explained, calm reassurance). Search demand + complete
  delivery = newcomer distribution without an audience.
- **End:** loops straight into his next tutorial ("watch that now") — session-time play.
- **Steal:** roadmap hook · anchor the title to a term people already search · be
  genuinely click-by-click · end by opening the next video.

## LOW — autopsy (139 views; OUR topic, competent, dead)
The script mechanics are fine (result-first open, next-video loop, comment prompt) —
which makes the failure instructive:
1. **Anchored to a zero-search term.** Title leads with "HyperFrames" — a tool nobody
   searches yet. Simon rode "HeyGen" (high search); MSG rode a word with no demand.
   *A 41-sub channel + no search anchor = no distribution path at all.*
2. **Jargon word-salad + premature CTA in the hook:** "Skills harness AI agents all in
   one personal mono repo. We're open sourcing it soon. **Subscribe so you don't miss
   the drop**" — at 0:18, before any value (the skill's #1 hook mistake, observed
   killing a real video).
3. **Me-centric framing:** "MY pipeline, MY bottleneck, MY workflow" — the viewer's
   payoff arrives late and thin (StoryBrand inversion: creator cast as hero, not guide).

## Cross-cutting (honest)
- **Both winners are face-cam channels** (frames confirm). We're faceless — their face
  is part of the trust signal we must replace with: the result-first artifact, own-voice
  VO, named honest limits, and shown iteration. (the Chronixel 8k→142k breakout proves
  small channels break out in this lane; face status unverified — do not overclaim.)
- Winners are LONG (18–32 min) — third confirmation of the long-form pattern in this
  lane (matches 2026-07-04 scrape finding).

## Conclusions → our script (each carries its evidence)
1. **Title anchors to "Claude" + a desirable RESULT** ("cinematic history video"),
   never to "HyperFrames" as the lead word (MSG autopsy + Simon's search win).
2. **Hook = our own artifact self-demonstrating** (play the 1907 liner / 1970 747
   scenes) + "no editor, no timeline" claim + roadmap (Nate's montage × Simon's roadmap).
3. **Give the starter away free** — our DESIGN.md template + exact prompts (Nate).
4. **No affiliate exists — say it out loud** as a trust beat (honesty moat; unique vs
   every grifter in the lane).
5. **Honest-limits section is the differentiator** (~55–65%): the cutback data (Claude
   can't cut real footage), our Playwright scroll-capture failure, and our own render's
   title-card flaw — shown, then fixed on screen (material variation for Gate 2).
6. **~15 min target** — evidence says 18–32 wins; we trade some length for a
   producible first video and structure so segments can expand later.
7. **End by opening the next video** (build-along / faceless money video) — Simon's loop.
