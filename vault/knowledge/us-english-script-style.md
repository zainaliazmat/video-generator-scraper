# Money Mavens — script style

> **BOX — the whole writing rule. Read the body only where it sends you.**
> 1. **Audience: Americans 50+**, deciding on Social Security claiming, Medicare
>    enrollment, RMDs, credit after 60, bank paperwork, and unfiled forms. If a line
>    works equally well for a 25-year-old in any English-speaking country, it is wrong.
> 2. **Five-part shape.** HOOK ≤25 words, no greeting, open on the thing itself ·
>    STAKES ~70 words naming the dollar amount or the deadline · PAYOFF 3-5 numbered
>    sections, each `claim → source on screen → worked example → what it means for you`,
>    ONE idea per section · PROOF woven through, ≥1 screenshot-the-source held 3s+ ·
>    CTA = ONE physical action, today, under 5 minutes, needing nothing they lack.
>  3. **Length: ~1,470 words for 9 min, ~1,640 for 10 min** (settled 2026-08-15 —
>    keep the voice, write longer; 140 wpm is retired). Budget from `format.json`
>    (`chars_per_second` 17.57 ÷ `chars_per_word` 5.63 = 3.121 words/s of audio) and
>    **subtract per-line padding from the target first** — 73.6 s of a LONG cut is not audio.
> 4. **Retired anchors** (they were written for a 25-year-old): "two DoorDash orders",
>    "transfer the day after payday", the mock-scold sign-off. Register is plain,
>    direct, respectful — never scolding, never chummy, never guilt.
> 5. **Say the term of art, then define it in the same breath** (IRMAA, RMD, SEP) —
>    the viewer will meet that word on a government form.
> 6. **Instant-reject localization:** `lakh` `crore` `rupee` `₹` · `petrol` `CV`
>    `mobile`(phone) `cheque` `flat` `queue` · `-ise`/`-our` spellings · ISA SIPP RRSP
>    TFSA PPF EPF SIP NPS superannuation · DD/MM/YYYY · metric · non-USD.
>    **Always:** US spelling, `$1,250.00`, MM/DD/YYYY, imperial, US institutions.
> 7. **Vault or `MISSING SOURCE:` — there is no third option.** Never a remembered number.
> 8. **Never:** invented stats · a decimal on the imprecise · named funds/banks/cards as
>    recommendations · emoji or ALL-CAPS in script · manufactured urgency · padding ·
>    calling the viewer a beginner · fear-mongering in place of a stated consequence.
>
> **Open the body when:** you need the wrong-vs-right anchor table, the wpm arithmetic,
> the full localization lists, or the compliance detail.

**Since 2026-08-15 this is the only finance lane, and it is aimed at Americans
50 and older.** One US/$ cut per video for @moneymavens101 ([[channels]] §E).

## Who is watching (creator repositioning, 2026-08-15)

Americans **at 50 and beyond**, facing decisions that are irreversible, deadline-
bound and expensive to get wrong:

- **Social Security claiming** — when to file, the reduction for filing early,
  spousal and survivor rules
- **Medicare enrollment** — IEP/GEP/SEP windows, Part B and D late-enrollment
  penalties, IRMAA
- **Required minimum distributions** — the age, the deadline, the penalty
- **Credit after 60** — what changes, what doesn't, what a thin file costs
- **Bank account paperwork** — beneficiaries, POD/TOD, joint accounts, holds
- **The forms that cost families thousands when nobody files them**

⚠️ **This is a different viewer from the one this note described until
2026-08-15.** The old anchors were written for a 25-year-old with a first
paycheck — "two DoorDash orders", "transfer the day after payday", "not there
when you're bored at midnight". **Retire all of them.** A 63-year-old choosing a
claiming age may have no payday at all, and a small-money anchor that reads as
cute to a 25-year-old reads as condescending to someone weighing a decision
worth six figures over their lifetime.

| Layer | ❌ wrong for this audience | ✅ Money Mavens |
|---|---|---|
| The stake | "you'll waste $1,596 a year" | **a one-time election that follows you for life** |
| The shock in the hook | layoff email, 22% credit card | **a missed enrollment window, a penalty that never goes away, a form nobody filed** |
| The time frame | "by next payday" | **a deadline with a date on it** |
| The small-money anchor | two DoorDash orders | **don't use one — quote the actual dollar consequence** |
| Who is in the story | "you, starting out" | **you, deciding — often for a spouse too** |
| The authority | Fed SHED, BEA, NY Fed | **SSA, Medicare, IRS, FDIC, CFPB** (see below) |
| The failure mode | overspending | **missing a window, or not knowing a rule existed** |

## The source rule — now a hard on-screen rule

The channel's own promise is *"with the source on screen"*. So:

**Every figure carries its issuing agency AND its publication date, on the frame
that shows the figure.** Not in the description, not in a closing card — on the
frame. A number without a visible source and date does not go on screen.

**The whitelist is [[fact-integrity]] §1 — sixteen agencies, and what each one is NOT
authoritative for.** It is not duplicated here; read it there. The short version: BLS ·
BEA · FRED/Fed · IRS · SSA · CMS/Medicare · Treasury · CFPB · FDIC/NCUA · SEC/investor.gov ·
FTC · Census · CBO · Fannie/Freddie · MSRB/EMMA · state treasurer/NAUPA, plus **FICO**
for the FICO score only.

A blog, an aggregator, or an AI summary is **not** a source — it is a lead to the primary
document. This is stricter than the general rule in [[evidence-discipline]] and it wins here.

**Why the date matters more on this channel than any before it:** these numbers
change on a published schedule. IRMAA brackets, Part B premiums, RMD tables and
the earnings-test threshold all move annually. An undated figure is not merely
unsourced — it will become *wrong* on a known date, on a video that keeps being
watched. Date every figure so a viewer in 2028 can see what year they are
looking at.

## Script architecture — the five-part shape

Every script follows this. Word counts below are for a **9-minute / ~1,470-word** cut;
for a 10-minute / ~1,640-word cut the extra words all go into PAYOFF — the hook, stakes
and CTA do not grow. See "Length" below for the arithmetic and the per-tier table.

| Part | Length | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **HOOK** | first 8 sec, ~25 words (fixed) | State the surprising fact or the stake. **No greeting, no "in today's video", no channel name, no "welcome back".** Open on the thing itself. |
| **STAKES** | next 30 sec, ~70 words (fixed) | Why this costs the viewer something. Name the dollar amount or the deadline. Say what they will know by the end. |
| **PAYOFF** | the body, **~1,255 words** (9 min) / **~1,425** (10 min) | Three to five numbered sections. Each: **claim → source on screen → worked example → what it means for you.** One idea per section — never braid two. |
| **PROOF** | woven throughout | Every figure appears with its source and as-of date visible. **At least one screenshot-the-source moment per video, held 3+ seconds.** |
| **CTA** | final ~120 words (fixed) | ONE specific physical action, doable today, in under 5 minutes, requiring nothing they do not already have. Then a one-line recap. Then the subscribe ask, once, plainly. |

> Good hook: *"There is a Medicare penalty that never goes away. Not for one year. For as
> long as you have Part B."*
> Bad hook: *"Hey everyone, welcome back to the channel. Today we're going to be talking
> about Medicare."*

> Good CTA: *"Today's one job: log in to your bank's website, find the beneficiary or
> payable-on-death section, and check whether a name is listed. If it is blank, that is
> the whole problem, and it takes ten minutes to fix at a branch."*

## Length — SETTLED 2026-08-15: keep the voice, write longer

**Creator decision: Brian stays at his measured rate and the scripts get longer.** The
plan's **140 wpm is retired as a budgeting input** — it describes a delivery pace this
voice does not have. Budget from `tools/format.json`, which now carries both constants:

| | value | where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| `cuts.en.chars_per_second` | **17.57** | measured on shipped MEDIUM/LONG renders |
| `script.chars_per_word` | **5.63** | measured 2026-08-15 across all seven shipped `-en` scripts (45,802 chars / 8,130 words) |

⇒ the voice delivers **3.121 words per second _of audio_**.

**Padding is charged per line and is not audio, so it comes off the target first.** That
subtraction is the entire difference between a script that passes gate one and one that
overruns — at 92 lines it is 73.6 seconds, well over a minute of runtime that carries no
words at all.

```
words = (target_seconds − lines × (lead_in + tail)) × chars_per_second ÷ chars_per_word
```

| Runtime | Tier | Lines | Padding | Audio | Chars | **Words** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8:30 | MEDIUM | 78 | 62.4 s | 447.6 s | 7,864 | **~1,400** |
| 9:00 | MEDIUM (in tolerance) | ~85 | 68.0 s | 472.0 s | 8,293 | **~1,470** |
| 10:00 | LONG | 92 | 73.6 s | 526.4 s | 9,249 | **~1,640** |

> **On the ~1,640 figure:** it is exactly right — for the **10-minute** cut, which renders
> at 9:59. For a **9-minute** video the padding-aware budget is **~1,470**, not 1,640;
> writing 1,640 words to a 9:00 target overshoots by about a minute and fails gate one at
> `length_tolerance_pct`. Five of the committed ten are 9 min and five are 10 min — check
> the video note's `length-min` before you start writing.

Pass/fail is ±`script.length_tolerance_pct` (10%) of the char budget, and `fin-audit`
enforces it by reading the file, never by re-deriving the rule.

## Localization — hard rules

**Always:** American spelling (`analyze`, `color`, `check`, `labor`, `program`) · USD as
`$1,250.00` (comma thousands, period decimal) · dates **MM/DD/YYYY** · imperial units ·
US institutions only (401(k), Roth IRA, Traditional IRA, HSA, FSA, FICO, W-2, 1099, HYSA,
Medicare, Social Security, FDIC, IRS, SSA, CFPB, FTC) · US seasonal money moments (tax
season, Medicare open enrollment Oct 15 – Dec 7, Black Friday, RMD deadline Dec 31 or
Apr 1 for the first one, back-to-school).

**Never — these are instant rejects:**
- `lakh`, `crore`, `rupee`, `₹`
- `petrol`, `CV`, `mobile` (say **cell phone**), `cheque`, `flat` (say **apartment**),
  `queue` (say **line**)
- `-ise` / `-our` spellings: `realise`, `colour`, `favour`, `organise`
- Any non-US institution: ISA, SIPP, RRSP, TFSA, PPF, EPF, SIP, NPS, superannuation,
  National Insurance
- DD/MM/YYYY dates, metric units, non-USD currency

**The US-specificity test:** if a line would work equally well for a 25-year-old in any
English-speaking country, it is wrong for this channel. Rewrite it. Every video note
carries a one-sentence `us-specific-angle` for exactly this reason.

## Negative constraints — do not

- Do NOT invent, estimate or "approximately recall" a statistic. A vault claim note or
  `MISSING SOURCE:` — there is no third option.
- Do NOT give personalized investment, tax or legal advice.
- Do NOT name specific securities, funds, banks, cards or apps as recommendations.
- Do NOT project or guarantee returns. The long-run market return is a **shape**, never a decimal.
- Do NOT use emoji in the script or the title. Do NOT use ALL-CAPS in the script.
- Do NOT use clickbait brackets in titles, or write a title the script cannot deliver.
- Do NOT manufacture urgency. If there is a real deadline, name the real deadline.
- Do NOT pad. If the script hits the point in 7 minutes, the video is 7 minutes.
- Do NOT address the viewer as a beginner, a young person, or someone starting out.
- Do NOT use second-person guilt ("you should have", "if only you had").
- Do NOT write anything that reads as fear-mongering rather than a stated consequence.

## The missing-source stop

**Only figures that exist as a claim note in `vault/claims/` may be used.** If a needed
number is not there, stop and emit:

```
MISSING SOURCE: [the exact claim you need]
Suggested primary source: [agency + document]
```

Do not proceed past a missing source. Do not substitute a number from memory. Full
evidence bar: [[fact-integrity]].

## Fixed decisions

- **Currency:** `$` everywhere, US comma grouping. Round numbers where the
  figure is illustrative; exact published figures where it is a rule.
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs **Brian** `nPczCjzI2devNBz1zQrb`, `eleven_multilingual_v2`,
  defaults. Voice id and rate live in `tools/format.json`, never here.
- **Speech rate ≈ 17.6 chars/s** for MEDIUM/LONG (`tools/format.json`; a SHORT
  cut runs ~9% long on that key — re-measure). Budget length before writing.
- **Titles + descriptions:** English.
- **Institutions named generically** for products: high-yield savings, checking,
  ACH. Never a specific bank, app, insurer or fund. **Agencies are named
  exactly** — SSA, Medicare, IRS — because they are the source, not a product.
- **Photography must match the audience.** People on screen read as 50+ where
  people appear at all. Sweep every photo for non-US currency, signage and
  vehicles, and for stock imagery that skews 25-35 — a young hand holding a
  phone under a Social Security line is the same defect class as a rupee note in
  a US cut.
- **Real dollar examples the viewer can run with their own numbers** (channel
  promise). Show the arithmetic and state the assumption in frame.

## Register

Plain, direct, respectful. Short sentences, one idea per line, no jargon — and
where a term of art is unavoidable (IRMAA, RMD, SEP) **say it, then define it in
the same breath**, because this viewer is going to meet that word on a
government form and needs to recognise it.

Not blunt, not scolding, and not chummy. The old mock-scold sign-off *"then
don't say nobody warned you"* is **retired** — it was written for a different
viewer and it reads as talking down to this one. Confidence here comes from
having the citation on screen, not from attitude.

Never imply the viewer has already made a mistake. Many of these decisions are
still open to the person watching; the ones that are not are usually not fixable,
and there is nothing to gain by rubbing it in.

## Compliance note

The channel's own About text ends: *"Educational content, not financial, tax, or
legal advice."* That matches the standing pipeline rules and raises the stakes:

- **No host persona, no claimed credentials.** [[niches/us-market-2026]]:
  YouTube's 2026 clarification makes AI "expert" personas in finance ineligible
  for monetisation regardless of added value. Narrate sourced facts; never say
  "as a financial advisor" or "I recommend".
- **No stock tips, no product recommendations** (the About text says so out
  loud). Products appear only as evidence of a price or a rule.
- **YMYL exposure is higher here than on the old positioning.** Social Security,
  Medicare and tax are the archetypal your-money-or-your-life topics. Every
  claim traceable to a primary agency document, dated, or it does not ship.
