# Subscription economics 2026 — India + USA

Research pass for video #3 «Needs vs Wants». Two versions ship from this note:
Hindi/India (₹) and English/USA ($). Confidence tags: **HARD** = primary or
top-tier press citing a primary study; **SOFT** = blog-tier, directional only,
never put on screen alone.

---

## 1. USA

### Income anchor
Salary/income figures live in [[money-facts-2026]] — **one home per fact, don't
duplicate them here.** The script uses that note's locked worked example:
**$4,000/mo take-home**. $1,596/yr against it = **12 days of pay**.

### The leak — the load-bearing stat
- **C+R Research (Apr–May 2022, n=1,000, via CNBC):** consumers *guessed* they spend **$86/month** on subscriptions. Walked through category by category, the real figure was **$219/month** — a **$133/month gap = $1,596/year**. **HARD**
- Same study: **42%** said they have forgotten they were still being charged for a subscription they no longer use. **HARD**
- ⚠️ The study is 2022. It is the most-cited hard number available and is directionally conservative in 2026 (prices rose since). Say "Americans" not "Americans in 2026"; do not re-date it.

### Streaming specifically
- **Deloitte Digital Media Trends 2026** (n=3,575, fielded Oct–Nov 2025, via Variety): US households spend **$69/month** on streaming video, flat YoY; average consumer holds **4 paid streaming services** (unchanged 3 years running); **~90%** of US households have at least one paid SVOD. **HARD**
- Same report: **61%** would cancel their favourite service on a **$5** price rise — i.e. the audience already feels this. Good hook-support. **HARD**

### Where the ghosts hide (US, for the audit scene)
iPhone → Settings → your name → Subscriptions · Google Play → Payments &
subscriptions · PayPal → Automatic payments · the card statement itself (annual
renewals only show once a year — that's why three months of statements isn't
enough for annuals).

### Numbers NOT to use
Every "$219 → $273 in 2026", "average American wastes $32/month", "Americans
spend $1,000/year on unused subs" figure surfaced in search traces back to
subscription-cancelling SaaS blogs with no methodology. **SOFT — do not use.**

## 2. India

### Income anchor
Same rule — salary figures live in [[money-facts-2026]] (PLFS 2025: ₹24,217 men
/ ₹18,353 women). The script uses that note's locked worked example:
**₹30,000/mo in-hand**. ₹24,564/yr against it = **25 days of pay**.

### Real subscription prices (published price cards, July 2026) — **HARD**
| Service | Price/month |
|---|---|
| Netflix Mobile / Basic | ₹149 / ₹199 |
| JioHotstar Super | ₹149 (₹79 mobile) |
| Spotify Premium (individual) | ₹119 |
| Google One 100 GB | ₹130 |
| Amazon Prime | ₹1,499/yr ≈ ₹125/mo |
| Food-delivery pass (Zomato/Swiggy tier) | ₹99–₹199 |
| Gym — basic local | ₹600–₹1,500 |
| Gym — mid-range w/ classes | ₹1,500–₹4,000 |

### The hero stack (what goes on screen)
Gym ₹1,500 + Netflix ₹199 + music ₹119 + cloud ₹130 + food pass ₹99
= **₹2,047/month = ₹24,564/year**.
Against the ₹30,000 in-hand worked example that is **0.82 of a month ≈ 25 days
of salary per year**. Every line item is a published price — this is why the
India math is built bottom-up instead of copying a survey average.

- ⚠️ **The creator's original brief (5 × ₹800 = ₹4,000/mo = ₹48,000/yr) does not hold at Indian prices.** ₹800 average per subscription is a US-shaped number. The bottom-up stack above is the honest replacement and is still a strong figure.

### Market context
- **Ormax OTT Audience Report 2025** (n=15,600, fielded Jun–Jul 2025, via IBEF): **601.2 mn** OTT users in India (41.1% of population); **148.2 mn** active paid subscriptions incl. telecom bundles. **HARD**
- **NPCI UPI AutoPay** is the Indian mechanism the whole video hangs on: standard mandate cap **₹15,000** without re-authentication (raised to ₹1 lakh for insurance / SIP / credit-card categories); recurring mandates growing ~35% YoY. **HARD**
- Where the ghosts hide (India, audit scene): **bank/UPI app → AutoPay / Mandates**, Google Play → Subscriptions, card statement, and telecom bundles (Jio/Airtel plans that silently include an OTT you already pay for separately — the most common Indian double-pay).
- SOFT/blog-tier only, do not put on screen: "3–7 active subscriptions per student", "youth subscription market grows 35%/yr (KPMG)", "young professionals spend 25% of income on fitness".

## 3. What transfers to the scripts

1. **US leads with a survey gap** ($86 guessed vs $219 real) — the drama is *self-deception*.
2. **India leads with a bottom-up stack** (₹2,047/mo itemised) — the drama is *addition*. Indian viewers price-check on screen instantly; a survey average would get called fake, an itemised list of real price cards cannot be.
3. Both land the same shape: one count-up to an annual number, then translated into **weeks of your own salary**.
4. Neither script names a product as a recommendation — platforms appear only as price evidence. (Educational-only rule.)
