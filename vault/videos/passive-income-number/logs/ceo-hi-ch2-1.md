### DECISION 2 — the stopping rule. ADOPTED as proposed, with one addition. BINDING ON ch3–7.

> **A relocated floor is a defect ONLY when the new bottom frame is (a) the chapter's PAYOFF
> frame or (b) the chapter's LONGEST-HELD frame. Otherwise the chapter stops.**

This is a clarification of the existing invariant, not a new rule: the invariant has always
been about **which** frame sits at the bottom, never about how deep the bottom is. A chapter
whose darkest frame is a dark photograph carrying a dry question has a floor; a chapter whose
darkest frame is its payoff has a defect. Without the terminating condition, every fix
relocates the bottom onto something and ch3–7 each pay a fetch round for a treadmill that has
already produced one wrong fix on this very chapter.

Applied here it clears: bottom on median is **s13 (21)** and **s10 (21)**, bottom on p10 is
**s19 (13)** — three dark photographs, none of them the payoff, none of them the longest-held.
s13 is a deliberately dry question, the second-shortest span, answered two scenes later by the
brightest frame in the chapter. Question dark → answer bright is the invariant working.

**THE ONE ADDITION, and it uses no new machinery:** the **binary sound-off gate**, which today
has standing only on the payoff frame, now also has standing on the **chapter's OPENING frame**.
With the type covered, a viewer must be able to name a concrete object in the chapter's first
frame. It is a gate, not a rank — I am deliberately not writing "the opening frame must be top
half on median", because that is exactly the flip-on-error rank test the payoff clause retired.
hi ch2's s9 **passes** the gate (a coin is nameable) and that is why this chapter ships; it
passes narrowly, which is why the finding below exists.
