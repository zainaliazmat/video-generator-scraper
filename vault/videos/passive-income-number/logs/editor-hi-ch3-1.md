## THE RULING QUESTION — a rank clause over a 0.06-point spread

**Ruled: the payoff clause is SATISFIED. s27 needs no fix.**

The measurement is not in dispute; fin-build's is the most careful on the run (complete
population, 1,752 frames, four span definitions, quantisation removed). The dispute is
whether it means anything. Median: s30 **31.30** · s22 **30.97** · s25 **30.94** · s27
**30.91**. Four photographs inside **0.39** points, the top three inside **0.06**.
p10: s29 21.96 · s30 19.97 · s27 **19.74** · s22 19.59 — the top-of-band spread is 0.38
once the one genuinely separated frame (s29, +2.22) is set aside.

**A rank clause cannot be decided by a separation smaller than the measurement's own
grain.** Two figures on record fix that grain at about one luma point: fin-build reports
that without in-bin interpolation *three scenes tie at an integer 31*, so 8-bit
quantisation alone is a 1.0-point step; and the CEO's own re-measurement of hi ch2's s16
p10 came back 24.0 where the editor had 23, a 1.0-point disagreement on a single frame
that the CEO itself called "inside method noise". Below 1.0 point the ordering is a coin
flip, and the coin has already flipped once here — fin-assets predicted s27 at #2/#2 with
absolute errors of only −0.99 and −1.26, and **both ranks inverted anyway**, because a
sub-point error is decisive in a 0.4-point band.

### Proposed qualifier — general, not a ch3 exception

> **A rank or superlative clause is decided only by separations that exceed the noise of
> the thing it measures. Frames within the noise band are TIED, and a rank clause is
> satisfied by any member of the tie. For the legibility measures the noise band is
> 1.0 luma point.**

Applied to ch3: on median the tie group is {s30, s22, s25, s27} at ranks 1–4, so s27 sits
in the group holding rank 1 and **top-quartile is satisfied**. On p10 s29 is genuinely #1
(+2.22, outside the band) and {s30, s27, s22} tie at ranks 2–4, so s27 sits in the group
holding rank 2 and **#1-or-#2 is satisfied**. Gate PASS and step-in +11.41 (the chapter's
largest) were never in doubt.

### The validity check, run the way the CEO required when it adopted the clause

A qualifier written to let one frame through is worthless, so I scored it against the
frames the clause exists to catch. **It changes no verdict reached on a real separation.**
en ch2's s21 sat at p10 15 in a chapter whose green scenes measured 43–50 — a ~25-point
gap, twenty-five times the band. hi ch2's hero still fails, as the clause's own adoption
test required. And this chapter's floor, s26 at median 19.49, is **4.98 clear of the next
frame up** and 11.4 below the top band; no tie band reaches it. The qualifier only ever
dissolves orderings that were never real.

It also costs this chapter something, which is how I know it is not self-serving: applied
to the superlative **"longest-held"**, s27 (8.976s) and s26 (8.219s) differ by 0.757s —
9%, plainly inside the variation of how long a voice takes to say a line — so they tie,
and fin-build's "s26 is the second-longest, so the stopping rule does not bite" stops
being available as a clean escape. It happens not to matter here (s26 is an original
floor, not a relocated one, and the stopping rule only governs relocated floors), but the
qualifier is pointing at this chapter, not away from it.

### Consistency with the two ruling-text defects already pending — all three are one rule

They should be taken together and they resolve into a single sentence.

1. **The stopping rule's "longest-held", decided on en ch3 by 0.76s of VO length.** Same
   disease, same site: a superlative resolving a difference smaller than what it purports
   to measure. The en editor's proposed fix — add "**OR an outlier against its own
   chapter**" — is my move in the other direction: it replaces *who ranks last* with *is
   the gap real*. Mine loosens a rank at the top of the distribution, the en editor's
   tightens one at the bottom, and both are the same principle: **rank out, separation
   in.** Adopt them as one clause, not two, and the third case (0.76s on ~8s) is covered
   by the general form rather than needing its own patch. The CEO's existing "a near-flat
   host frame is not a valid comparator on the p10 clause" then becomes a *worked example*
   of the general rule rather than a standing special case — worth folding in so the
   ruling stops accreting exceptions.

2. **The invariant's sentence stating its own converse — and it has now happened, twice,
   in this chapter's own logs.** `ground_and_payoff_legibility_2026-08-08` reads "the
   darkest longest-held frame must be the chapter's most substantive beat". **fin-assets
   wrote "the invariant is satisfied BY CONSTRUCTION" and fin-build wrote the same, both
   by observing that ch3's darkest frame happens to be a substantive beat** (s26, the May
   argument). Read literally the text agrees with them. Read as the s21 and s31 blockers
   actually enforced it, the requirement is the opposite one — *a substantive beat must
   not be left in the darkest frame* — and under that reading s26 is a question to answer,
   not a box already ticked. The en ch3 editor predicted the text "can be quoted to
   justify the exact defect it was written to stop"; this chapter is the third instance
   and the first where it produced two independent agents ticking the box. **Rewrite it to
   the enforced direction and only that direction.** Suggested wording: *the chapter's
   darkest frame must not be one of its load-bearing beats.*

   For the record, so the rewrite does not silently create a new blocker here: under the
   corrected direction s26 **is** the chapter's darkest frame and **is** load-bearing, and
   its 4.98-point gap to the next frame is real, not noise. I am not raising it, and the
   reason is the CEO's own sharpening — the binary sound-off gate is the floor and the
   numbers only rank. s26 passes the gate without hesitation (a rusted kWh meter, dials
   and KILOWATT-HOURS legible, sitting directly behind the type), its subject is the
   brightest thing in its own frame, and its p10 (15.18) is inside a four-frame tie with
   s23/s24/s25 rather than alone at the bottom. Its median is low because the meter sits
   in a large dark surround, which is a framing property and not a legibility one. Calling
   it a defect would be letting a number overrule the eye at the bottom of the chapter
   while I argue against exactly that at the top. But the CEO should decide it knowingly
   rather than inherit "satisfied by construction".
