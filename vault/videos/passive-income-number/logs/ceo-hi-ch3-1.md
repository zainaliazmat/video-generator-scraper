# CEO · passive-income-number · hi · chapter 3 · attempt 1 (REFERRED RULING, s29 only)

**VERDICT: RULING — option (b). This is not a chapter verdict.** Chapter 3 stays REWORK
on the editor's two blockers; I did not review the other eight scenes and finding 1 (s22),
finding 3 (s24's drawn layer) and findings 4–7 are untouched by this ruling.

## The ruling

**Hold the never-a-screen rule. It is unamended, and it is binding on s52 and on every
later scene that names the internet. Nothing here grants a screen, so nothing reopens s4
or s52.** No exception exists to be quoted at a later slot.

## Why the collision was not real

The rule the storyboard actually writes is **"Never a *lit* phone-screen photo as a
background"** (§Overrides s4, l.952; the same wording at l.765). A **dark, switched-off**
screen is APPROVED and has shipped four times — s1, s4, s74, s75 — and the storyboard
itself says that is "what makes it a decision and not an exception." s52's stricter form
("no phone screen and no monitor in frame") is a **scene-local tightening**, not the
general rule, and it exists for a reason specific to s52: s52's beat is *what the
internet shows you*, so any screen there would have to be lit and legible, i.e. a
fabricated results page, which `truth_bar` bans on top of everything else.

So there was never a rule saying s29 may not have a screen in it. What there is, is that
**a screen cannot help s29**: a dark one does not say *internet* (fin-assets ranked its
own exit 3 last for exactly this, and it was right), and a lit one collides with three
separate bans at once — the lit-background ban, the fabricated-legible-source ban, and
s29's own brightness constraint (it must land **below p10 21.96**; a lit screen under a
`.stmt` block with a ken sweep is the brightest background family the system has).

**The real cause of the unsatisfiable brief is elsewhere, and both agents made the same
move.** The editor reasoned "non-screen → the storyboard's approved non-screen answer for
the internet is print (s52)", and fin-assets then spent seven sheets inside the print
family. But s52's answer is print *because s52 needs the search-results layout*, and s29
does not. Collapsing "non-screen" onto "print" imported s52's requirement into a scene
that never had it, and then s29's own no-confusable-with-s52 clause deleted the only
thing that made print work. That is a self-inflicted bind, not a pool failure — and
fin-assets diagnosed it precisely ("asking the pools for the one thing left over after
its own distinguishing feature has been ruled out"). Both agents were correct within the
frame they inherited; the frame was wrong.

**The internet has a physical body that is neither a screen nor a page.** Nobody fetched
it.

## The subject, concrete enough to fetch

**Primary: the overhead cable/fibre tangle on an Indian street pole — the pole filling
the frame, shot at dusk or under overcast, no bright sky band, no signage, no
transformer, no bird.**

Why this one and not another:

- It **says internet sound-off**, in India, immediately and without the type. It is the
  most-photographed object in the Indian streetscape that means *the line into your
  house*.
- It carries **the second half of the line too**. A knot of two hundred wires where six
  would do *is* visual over-provision — "bigger than it is" is the object's own meaning,
  not something the type has to lend it. The horn speakers had amplification and no
  internet; print had neither; this has both. No still photograph carries *faster* —
  the `stmt:` carries faster, and that split is honest.
- It **reuses the geometry that already worked**. Attempt 1's frame is a pole against
  sky at p10 21.96 on the chapter's warn ground `#2b1418`. Same composition, same street
  register, same darkness, subject swapped. This is the cheapest possible re-fetch and
  the layout is already proven in the encode.
- **No text, no brand, no currency, no face, no hand.** The four bans that killed
  attempt 1's sheets do not reach it.
- **Brightness is with us, not against us.** The print family pushed white (r3 cells 1,
  2, 5 near-white); a pole-filling dusk frame lands under 21.96 naturally. Hold that
  constraint: reject any cell with a bright sky band, and reject the bright-sky variant
  the way attempt 1 correctly rejected the cyan-sky horn.

Queries for fin-assets: `tangled overhead cables on pole dusk`, `messy electrical wires
utility pole city`, `bundle of internet cables street pole`, `cable clutter wires pole
evening`. Pexels first (the `min_width_px` floor).

**In-family fallback, if the pole family refuses: a home wifi router/modem, antennas up,
status LEDs lit, photographed from the rear or three-quarter so no badge face is legible,
dark surround.** It is a genuinely different object family, so a failure there is a new
finding and not a third bite at the same one. It reads *internet* even harder than the
cables but carries none of the excess, so it is second, not first. Brand marks are the
live risk (the `TOA` kill is the precedent) — check at full resolution, not on the sheet.

## The ch2 `s17` RJ45 kill does not reach this

It will be raised, so: §10a rejected a bare RJ45 lead under *the phone recharge and the
home internet* for "neither subject present". That line's subjects were a recharge and a
bill — money objects — and a lead is neither. Here the line's subject **is** the internet,
and the distinction that matters is scale and nameability: a single lead on a desk is an
unnameable component; an overhead bundle on a pole is a place-object a viewer names on
sight. That is a real difference, not a dodge.

## Why not (c), the drawn layer — refused, with reasons

- s29 is the chapter's **shortest scene at 5.398s**, and it sits **immediately after
  s28**, the chapter's `art-forward` `corpus-doubles`. Two consecutive drawn beats on the
  chapter's two shortest-to-densest stretch is where the strip starts reading
  constructed.
- It is the **foil**. Giving the chapter's weakest claim its most built, most authored
  frame is the same argumentative inversion the ground/payoff rule already polices on
  this exact scene — and fin-assets already invoked it here to reject the bright horn
  variant. Do not spend craft on the sentence you are arguing against.
- The drawn layer exists for propositions a photograph **structurally cannot** assert
  (`vector_art.lottie.reach_for_it_when`; the s4 banner is the model). The cable tangle
  asserts this one. The precondition is not met.
- Slot arithmetic, for the record: this would be **3 of 4** with the editor's s24 proposal
  at 2. I am not spending the third here. The s24 proposal (finding 3) is a separate
  decision and this ruling neither grants nor refuses it.

## Binding statements, so this does not get re-litigated

1. The never-a-screen rule **stands as written** and is **binding on s52** — s52 keeps
   its printed-search-results brief, no phone screen, no monitor in frame.
2. It is **binding on every later scene that names the internet**. The reason it costs
   s52 nothing and cost s29 nothing is that the two scenes name the internet for
   different reasons: s52 needs what the internet *shows* (which requires the results
   layout, which only print supplies), s29 needs what the internet *does* (which does
   not). Later scenes route by that test, not by copying s29's answer.
3. **"Non-screen" is not "print".** Recorded so the next brief does not re-derive the
   bind: print is s52's answer to a layout requirement, not the cut's general answer to
   the internet.
4. The fetch inherits the **p10 < 21.96** constraint and attempt 1's four vocabulary
   bans.
