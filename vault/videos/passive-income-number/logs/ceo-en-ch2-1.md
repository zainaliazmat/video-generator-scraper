## THE CROSS-CUT RULING — settled once, binds ch3–6 (en) and ch3–7 (hi)

I was asked whether en's declared `--fund` green ground is an **exception** to the hi ch1
invariant or a **violation** of it. It is **neither, because the premise is wrong.**

**Measured, not argued.** The eight ground hexes in this chapter, as relative luma:

**`--fund` #12351f is the LIGHTEST ground in the chapter** — ten points above the amber that
carries the entire evidence body. And the encode agrees: the four green-role scenes measure
p90 **50.7 (s18) · 43.7 (s21) · 46.0 (s22) · 44.0 (s23)**, with s18 *above* the chapter's
49.4 weighted average. A green ground cannot be what drags the hero to the floor when it is
the brightest ground present and one of its own scenes is the fourth-brightest in the chapter.

**What actually puts the hero at the floor is s21's photograph.** p10 15 — tied with s20 for
the chapter's darkest tenth — under a global `.bg` grade of `grayscale(.32) brightness(.62)`
that no per-scene knob overrides. The `--tint` is `rgba(FUND, .10)`, a 10% radial. It is not
doing this. A dark source is.

**So there is no declared device to grant an exception to.** en's inversion has the *same*
cause as hi's — an under-exposed photograph under the payoff — wearing a declared colour
role as an alibi. The two cuts did not arrive at one defect by two routes. They arrived at
it by one route, twice.

**RULING, binding on both cuts from ch3 forward:**

1. **The hi ch1 invariant stands, unqualified. There is no `--fund` exception.** Ground
   temperature is assigned by argumentative weight, not mood; the darkest longest-held frame
   must be the chapter's most substantive beat and the brightest must not be its emptiest.
2. **The operable form, which is the half that was missing and is what a fetch brief can
   actually act on: the payoff frame's photograph must be the most legible in its chapter,
   never the least.** Light is how a viewer knows a beat matters. A corpus number is the only
   thing a rung chapter exists to deliver, so its picture is the one that must survive
   `brightness(.62)`.
3. **The lever is the PHOTOGRAPH. Never the ground hex, never `.scrim`, never the grade.**
   s20 proved this empirically inside this very chapter: one swap moved that scene's p90 **+8**
   and its p10 **+0**. Ground hexes and scrims own the shadows; the photograph owns the
   highlights, and the highlights are what a viewer reads as brightness. Re-grading is
   forbidden as a tone fix in both cuts — it greys every still to move a number.
4. **hi ch2's target is therefore UN-INVERTED, not brighter** — re-fetch the frame under its
   hero and the frame under its 59.0 blank-notebook plateau. Raising hi ch2's weighted average
   without moving those two files fixes the number and leaves the defect, exactly as the hi ch1
   gate predicted.
5. **This is a FETCH-BRIEF rule, not a check.** Per the standing fix-ladder: every corpus scene
   in ch3–6 (en) and ch3–7 (hi) is briefed for a source that is the chapter's brightest and most
   legible object, and fin-assets rejects a corpus candidate on YHIGH before it rejects it on
   anything else. Do not add a gate that tells each chapter it got it wrong again.

**en ch2 is in violation of clause 2 and that is blocker 1 below.** Note it does *not* violate
the invariant's other half — en's two brightest frames are s16 (59.0, the 95/100 grid) and s19
(59.0, `$10,169`), both substantive, and its darkest longest-held frame is s10 (43.0, 11.05s),
the tank analogy that is load-bearing again at 4.7 and 5.5. That half is correct and I am not
asking for it to change. en's defect is narrower and more specific than hi's: not "light lands
on the empty frame" but "light drains off the payoff".
