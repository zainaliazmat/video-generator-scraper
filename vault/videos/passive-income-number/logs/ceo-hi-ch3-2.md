## Would I keep watching?

Yes — from **0:18 onward**, comfortably. The back half is the best run of frames on this cut:
s26's rusted meter under *"The bill did not shrink. The pocket changed."* is the chapter's
argument in one picture, s27's amber stamp desk is a proper payoff, s28's doubling bars are the

**The timestamp I would leave at is 0:11.5–0:18.1** — the second half of the abacus hold.
I watched the run of it rather than the cells: the chapter opens well (gullaks fill the frame,
coin slits countable, ₹20,00,000 lands), then hands the viewer **12.63 seconds of one
photograph** — 20.6% of the chapter — across the two beats where they are being asked to do
arithmetic. The ken is 1.00→1.16 over that whole span, i.e. visually near-static. The abacus
beads are not set to any value and never move, so for the length of both operations the picture
is asserting nothing and the type is carrying 100% of the load.

That span is also where clarity thins. A first-time viewer sees ₹20,00,000 → ₹60,000 → ₹5,000
in three steps, and **the ÷12 — the only step they cannot check in their head — exists on
screen as a 20px grey footnote**, «₹60,000 divided by 12 · ILLUSTRATIVE ARITHMETIC». At 1.5×
on a phone that line is not read. The chapter's most-repeated teaching is its least-shown one.

**This does not read flat as a strip**, and I want that on the record so the fix is not
mis-scoped. Ground temperature moves and lands correctly: cold s25 → warm s26/s27/s28 with the
hottest ground (amber) on the thesis at s27 → cold s29/s30 on the foil and the landing.
Archetype rhythm is **A B B C A B B C A** — symmetric, not collapsed. Luma runs 27·26·27·31·19·31·28·23·31.
Nine distinct objects. **The fix is not another layout. It is one scene given something real to
hold**, which is exactly what the editor proposed and what §8's own rule 8 test licenses.
