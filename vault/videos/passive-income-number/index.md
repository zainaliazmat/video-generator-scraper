## The durable lessons

1. ⚠ **Gate two is the ONLY stage that ever watches the assembled master MOVE. Never cut it.**
   §3b says its unique job is sampling inside the cross-dissolves. True, and an undersell: every
   other check upstream reads either a chapter or a still. It caught a master that rendered as
   81 static slides after `hyperframes check`, a 2fps luminance probe, six locked chapters and a
   creator frame sign-off had all passed it. The tempting argument for skipping it — "every
   scene has already been seen" — is exactly the reasoning that would have shipped this.
2. **A checker that asks "did it run / is it well-formed" cannot see a thing that never ran.**
   Fourth instance on this one video, after `stamp warn` red-on-red, `@commons` returning an
   unbounded original and `fade()` as a silent no-op. All four produced a **well-formed wrong
   result** rather than an error. `hyperframes check` still reports `Motion ◇ 0 errors` on both
   the broken and the fixed file. **Only a count against the source, a composed frame, or an eye
   can see an absence.**
3. **When a warning is about CAPTURE behaviour rather than content, render the whole thing at a
   low frame rate and measure it.** The 162-overlay warning sat open from ch2 for a week. Because
   the failure is presence-based and duration-independent, a full-length **2 fps** draft
   reproduced the entire overlay tree in 1,056 frames — **2m 15s** — and settled it: zero black
   segments, min YAVG 36.97, first half 47.30 vs second half 45.72. ⚠ **But scope the probe
   honestly**: that same probe was blind to the missing motion, because a deck of stills produces
   a perfectly healthy luminance profile. A probe answers only the question it was built for.
4. **In R3, apply the constraint filter BEFORE sorting on score.** 3 of 10 generated titles broke
   a `run.json` constraint — all three on the same bare `$5,000/Month` with no rate — and one of
   them scored **85**. An unshippable string should never anchor the decision. (fin-package's own
   recommendation; adopt it in the recipe.)
5. **Re-read `vidiq_balance` — it is free — and never carry a plan-level number forward in a
   note.** This run carried "only 13 credits remain, ration it or defer the title lock" from
   2026-08-07 and was about to buy a deliberately degraded packaging pass. The real balance was
   **5,453**. The whole R3+R3b pass cost 25.
6. **A name test is not an identity test.** The assembler linked the cut's `assets/img/` "because
   it exists"; it holds a `manifest.json` of sourced photographs, a different thing wearing the
   same name as the design system's `img/` (grain, watermark). That one link 404'd the fonts,
   gsap, lottie and the watermark.
7. ⚠ **`check render` is CUT-level, so a per-clip timing defect cannot surface until the whole
   cut is assembled and rendered** — the most expensive possible moment. Line 4.8's VO drift
   (+0.139s, 39 ms over target) has been sitting in ch4 since it was built, reviewed and locked;
   ch4's own draft measures +0.134s. A chapter's gates are `hyperframes check` and a draft
   render, and **neither measures drift**. Raised as
   `owed.vo_drift_uses_one_global_onset_constant`.
8. **The keyword earns its place, measurably.** The locked title minus the single word
   "Invested" scores **81** instead of 87. And `passive income` — 346,954 searches/month — is a
   trap on a US cut at **8.1% US share** (PK 21.3%, IN 11.0%); `dividend investing` is the real
   anchor at 48,704 US/month and the lowest competition in the set.
   ⚠ Note the drift this exposes: the slug says `passive-income-number`, but the title, the
   demand and the tags all live in the **dividend** family.

## What broke

- ⚠⚠ **THE MASTER RENDERED WITH NO MOTION AT ALL — 81 static slides that merely cross-dissolved.
  Cost: one wasted 29m 37s render plus the 57m 19s re-render.** Root cause in
  `cut_assemble.py`'s `handbuilt()`: `html.rindex("<script>")` takes the LAST inline script.
  Correct for the hand-built chapters it was written for, which keep their timeline in one
  block — but a `build.mjs` chapter emits **two**, the motion timeline (11,428 chars in ch2) and
  then the RATE ASSERT (4,006). The merge took the assert; `take_rate_assert()` then correctly
  lifted it out for dedup, leaving every chapter contributing an empty body. On screen: every
  cue landed at t=0 — ch1 s2's chips all present at 4.10s instead of cascading, ch2 s16 static
  at `95%` where the draft is mid-count at `88%`, ch4 s46's tank already full. **Fixed** (walk
  every inline script) **and guarded** (`verify_motion`, 382 carried / 382 declared).
  ⚠ The creator sign-off could not have caught it: `PREVIEW.mp4` is 528.085s — the exact sum of
  the six chapter drafts, written three days before the master existed — so it is a concat of
  the CHAPTERS, and a still frame cannot show a missing *arrival* at all.
- **`cut_assemble.py` scaffolding assumed a cut directory a chapter-first run never creates** —
  `package.json` and `node_modules` died in `shutil.copy`. Now resolved by looking, with an
  error that names the fix.
- **Six copies of the RATE ASSERT spliced into one master, and ch1's was STALE** — it predates
  the `derived_income_carries_assumption` extension and lacks the BILL branch. A subset, so it
  could not have thrown falsely here, but a stale assert would only ever surface during the
  full render. Deduped to one, the strictest.
- **`tools/transcript.py` had the same chapter-first blind spot** — it looked only for
  `<slug>-<cut>/index.html`. Now falls back to the assembled `-full/`, and its provenance line
  names the file it actually read.
- **`verify_motion` itself was wrong on its first run** (`ken 77/78, pop 55/58 …`). It was
  counting prose comments that *describe* motion, in blocks the merge legitimately drops. It
  strips comments now and compares code to code. Recorded because the fix was found by running
  the guard against real files, not by reasoning about it.
- **VO drift +0.139s at line 4.8 — RESCUED, not fixed.** 39 ms over a 100 ms target, one line of
  81, bit-identical across two independent renders, and present in ch4's locked draft. Re-cutting
  the clip costs a full re-render to move a sound no listener can locate; nudging its placement
  would desync it from its scene to satisfy a measurement; tuning the threshold to pass was
  rejected outright as threshold-fitting to our own output.

   `fact_gate.disclaimer_placements` requires all four of spoken · onscreen · description ·
   pinned. This render has **zero** hits for "advice", "education", "licensed" or
   "professional", so only **description and pinned** are satisfied. ⚠ **`spoken` is
   structurally unreachable on this video** — it needs a new VO line, which changes
   `timing.json` and invalidates all six locked chapters and the render. So **4/4 is impossible
   here no matter what is decided**; the only choice is 2/4 or 3/4 (one more ~57-minute render
   for an on-screen lower third). ⚠ **`fact_gate` and `fact-integrity.md` were committed
   2026-08-15 (`a51d02d`); this script is dated 2026-08-07** — the standard is eight days
   younger than the video. Decide ONCE, at channel level, and write it into
   `fact-integrity.md` — whether pre-standard cuts are grandfathered — rather than re-arguing
   it on every video in flight.
2. ⚠ **The 128-cue mix has never been heard by anyone.** `mix.py` lays SFX AFTER the render, so
   every chapter was locked on sight and voice alone. The cut carries **128 cues against a
   `max_sfx_cues` budget of 30** — 80 of them `transition`, one on literally every scene
   boundary, roughly one sound every 6.6 seconds. Nothing enforces that budget in code. Weigh it
   at the proof-listen against `format.json`'s own argument: *"a sound is punctuation, and if
   every reveal has one then none of them means anything."* Re-mixing is one command and needs
   **no** re-render.
3. **The AI-enhance prompt is plate-only and title-independent**, so the title lock did not
   stale it. The thumbnail was re-checked against the new title and deliberately KEPT — the
   figure-free title turns the tile's `$1.5M at 4.0%` / `$5.6M at 1.08%` into the answer to the
