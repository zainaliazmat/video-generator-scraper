## Hero math (both cuts — arithmetic, no source needed)

- India: **₹12,000/mo auto-moved on the 1st → ₹1,44,000/yr.** Pure arithmetic.
- US worked example (locked in money-facts-2026): $4,000/mo take-home → 10% =
  **$400/mo → $4,800/yr**. Deliberate echo: $400/mo saved vs the $400 emergency
  ~4 in 10 US adults can't cover (SHED row below) — same number, two fates.

⚠ **India hero-plausibility flag (script stage must handle):** PLFS 2025 avg
salaried earnings are **₹24,217/mo (men) / ₹18,353 (women)** — verify-only,
HARD, [[knowledge/money-facts-2026]]. ₹12,000/mo saved is ~50% of the average
salary — implausible for the median viewer. Frame ₹12,000 as the
higher-earner example ("₹60–80k in-hand at 15–20%") OR present the ladder:
5% of ₹24,000 ≈ **₹1,200/mo ≈ ₹14,400/yr** (matches the "even 5%" action
step), scaling up to ₹12,000 for higher incomes. Never imply ₹12,000 is what
the average salary supports.

## Rule provenance (both cuts)

"Pay yourself first" as a principle traces to **George S. Clason, *The Richest
Man in Babylon* (1926)** — "a part of all you earn is yours to keep," start
with 10%. Multiple book sources concur (jamesclear.com/book-summaries/the-richest-man-in-babylon,
readingraphics.com, richestman.org). Safe to state on screen: "a 100-year-old
rule." HARD (provenance, not a money claim). The *automation* twist (payday
auto-transfer) is modern convention — don't attribute it to Clason.
