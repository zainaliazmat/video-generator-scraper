## en3 — THE HONEST TEST

**VO**
> First, the test. A need is what you can't survive without — rent, groceries, insurance, gas, your medication. A want is what makes life good — streaming, takeout, the new phone. Wants aren't the enemy. Just stop calling them needs.

**VO**
> Second — the twenty-four hour rule. Anything non-essential: put it in the cart and wait a day. If it still matters tomorrow, buy it. Seven times out of ten you'll forget it existed. And if it's over a hundred dollars, wait thirty days instead.

## en6 — CUT WITHOUT MISERY

**VO**
> Fourth — cut, but don't make your life boring. Ask every subscription one question: how many times did I use this in the last thirty days? Zero? Cancel it. Once? Drop to the ad-supported tier, or split it with family. And the one thing you use every single day — keep it, guilt free. Then one rule forever: one new subscription in, one old subscription out.

**On screen**
- kicker: `Ask one question`
- huge: `USED IT IN THE LAST` + fundc `30 DAYS?`
- decision rows: `ZERO → CANCEL` · `ONCE → AD TIER / SHARE` · `DAILY → KEEP IT`
- stamp (fund): `ONE IN, ONE OUT`
- foot: `61% would quit their favorite service over a $5 hike — Deloitte 2026`

**Visuals** — Pexels: `person cancelling on laptop`, `family watching tv living room`.

## en7 — THE MATH (count-up)

**VO**
> Now the real math. Streaming alone runs the average American household sixty-nine dollars a month, across four services. Then add the gym, the apps, the cloud storage. Asked to guess their total, Americans said eighty-six dollars. Counted category by category, the real number was two hundred nineteen. That gap — a hundred and thirty-three dollars every month — is one thousand five hundred ninety-six dollars a year you never counted. On a four thousand dollar take-home, that's twelve days of pay.
