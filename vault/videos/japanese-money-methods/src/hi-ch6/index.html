KAKEIBO

The household account book. Written by hand.

1904

Hani Motoko. Japan's first woman journalist.

Fujin no Tomo Sha (publisher primary) + National Diet Library. First edition printed end-1904, for use in the 1905 year.

STILL PRINTED

Every year since. Four wartime years missing.

Over a hundred years of continuous publication — the publisher marked the 120th in 2025

HER ACTUAL INVENTION

Not the four-column chart. The budget.

THE METHOD

Divide the year by twelve. Set the saving aside FIRST. Then split the rest.

WHY IT WAS NEW

Saving is decided before spending, not after.

THE FOUR CATEGORIES

These came later. They are the version that travelled west.

Consistent across secondary sources; no Japanese primary attributes them to Hani Motoko or to 1904

SAY IT PLAINLY

Her own expense heads were different ones.

WHAT SURVIVES

Four questions. Asked at the start of the month.

THE FOUR

How much came in · How much is set aside · How much goes out · What changes from last month

THE ORDER IS THE METHOD

Question two comes before question three.

ON PAPER

Pen. Notebook. Phone face-down. Fifteen minutes.

APP vs LEDGER

An app reports where it went. A kakeibo decides where it goes.
