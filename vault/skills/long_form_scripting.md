# SKILL — Long-Form YouTube Scripting & Delivery (v1)
 
**Companion to `youtube_channel_skill.md`.** That skill decides *whether* to make a video (money, research, Gate 0/1, compliance). **This skill is how the video gets written and delivered** so it's structurally engineered for retention, psychologically grounded, and never generic.
 
**Load this when:** Gate 1 has passed and it's time to write or revise a long-form (~8+ min) script.
 
---
 
## 0. CHANNEL CONTEXT + PRIME DIRECTIVE
 
- **Channel:** faceless AI-tools-for-creators — tool reviews & comparisons ("I tested X so you don't have to"), automation/workflow how-tos ("make faceless videos with AI"), and specific-tool tutorials. Visuals = **screen recordings** + b-roll/graphics (no face on camera).
- **Voice:** the **creator's OWN recorded human voice** over those visuals. This is real VO, not text-to-speech. (ElevenLabs is a tool we *review* and our primary affiliate — it is not how we narrate.) Everything in §8 Delivery assumes a human at a mic.
- **Default formats:** action-oriented (reviews, comparisons, "best X for Y", "is X worth it", tutorials). These are where newcomers win and where affiliate/sponsor money lives. **Do NOT default to a neutral explainer** — that lane is giant-dominated and low-intent.
- **Prime directive:** every script is **engineered**, not assembled. A generic "intro → here are the tools → outro" loses viewers. Each script must have an open-loop hook, a deliberate retention shape, a clear point of view with honest verdicts, an analogy for every abstract idea, and a delivery layer marked onto the page.

## 1. NON-NEGOTIABLE SCRIPTING PRINCIPLES
 
1. **Money-first format, value delivered fast.** Action formats are default. **Don't withhold the answer** — viewers came for the decision, so front-load the punchline/overview, then go deep. (Withholding is only for the rare explainer that Gate 1 proved a newcomer can win — see §3.) Always close with an **honest verdict + affiliate CTA**, framed honestly.
2. **Honesty is the moat.** Give clear "worth it / skip" calls. Say out loud when a **free tool wins** and when you **earn no commission** on a pick — that candor is trust a giant can't fake. **Never shill a worse product for a bigger commission.** Recommend by quality first, then monetize the honest pick.
3. **The "I tested" integrity rule.** If the script claims hands-on testing ("I tested", "in my testing"), the human MUST actually run that test before publishing — or soften to "here's what testing shows / what creators report." Fabricated test claims are the fastest way to sink a review channel. This is also what keeps the channel "materially varied" and authentic for Gate 2.
4. **Write for the ear, and for YOUR voice.** Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Contractions. Spoken rhythm. Cut any sentence you can't say comfortably in one breath. Read every line aloud as you write it — if you stumble, the sentence is wrong, not your delivery. (This also satisfies plain language: Feynman-simple, second person, define jargon the instant you use it.)
5. **Accuracy / legal.** For buyer's-guide/tutorial/comparison content, "facts" = accurate specs, current pricing, feature comparisons, hands-on behavior. Date-stamp and triangulate (≥2 sources). Speak prices as approximate ("around $X as I record this — check the live price via the link"); put precise dated figures in the fact-check table, not the VO. Truth is a complete defense; "in my opinion" doesn't shield a false factual claim — attribute harsh claims about named companies to lawsuits/critics/your own testing. Disclose affiliate relationships.
6. **Design for compliance (Gate 2).** Every video must carry your own testing, angle, voice, and POV — that material variation is what keeps the *whole channel* monetizable. Use cleared music only.

## 2. LENGTH, PACING & THE END-TO-END BUILD
 
**Pacing math.** Spoken pace ≈ **150 words per minute** (conversational adult English; ~130–160 range). So: 8 min ≈ 1,200 words, 10 min ≈ 1,500, 12 min ≈ 1,800. Long-form = 10+ min unlocks mid-roll ads; a common engagement sweet spot is ~8–15 min. **Write the first draft, then cut it ~30%** — the first draft is always too long. Read aloud and time it.
 
**The build, in order:**
1. **Outline first — never a blank page.** Spine: **Hook → Setup → Body segments → Payoff → CTA.** Put a rough timestamp on each act *before* writing, to prevent the filler middle (the #1 cause of mid-video drop-off).
2. **Two-track / two-column script** is mandatory because we're faceless — *the visuals carry the show.* Left column = **VO** (what you say). Right column = **`[ ]` on-screen cue** (screen recording, b-roll, graphic, zoom, text overlay). Every line gets a planned visual.
3. **Default action-format arc for this channel:** Hook (open a loop) → promise the exact decision/payoff → fast overview or early verdict → segmented deep-dive (one tool/step per segment, each with an example + an honest verdict card) → the "big one" payoff near ~70% → final verdict + CTA.
4. **Chapters** built into the structure (not bolted on in the description).

## 3. PROVEN STRUCTURES & STORY ARCS (interchangeable skeletons)
 
Pick by intent; all are compatible with the arc in §2.
 
- **Open-loop / curiosity-gap:** open an unresolved question early, keep it open, close it late. Stack 2–3 loops in a long video. *The backbone of retention.*
- **PAS — Problem → Agitate → Solve:** the default for how-to/educational. Name the creator's pain, make it sting, then resolve. (e.g., "Editing a faceless video by hand takes hours → here's why it kills your output → the tool that cuts it to minutes.")
- **AIDA — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action:** persuasive/CTA-driven videos.
- **ABT — "And, But, Therefore"** (the causality test, from Trey Parker/Matt Stone via Randy Olson): Context (*and*) → conflict (*but*) → consequence (*therefore*). **Run this on every transition** — if beats connect with "and then," it's a list; if they connect with "but"/"therefore," it's a story.
- **BAB — Before → After → Bridge:** transformation framing; the bridge is the tool/method.
- **Dan Harmon Story Circle (8 beats: You → Need → Go → Search → Find → Take → Return → Change):** for personal/journey videos ("how I built a faceless channel with $0").
- **Pixar story spine** ("Once upon a time… Every day… Until one day… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since then…"): case studies and narrative explainers.
- **Three-act / Freytag:** any narrative video; map acts to timestamps.
- **Explainer structure (use sparingly — only if Gate 1 proved a newcomer can win):** hook → withhold the core → reveal → thesis kicker (Loewenstein curiosity gap). For this channel, default to action formats instead.

## 4. HOOKS & THE FIRST 30 SECONDS
 
**The hook's three jobs, in order:** (1) **confirm the packaging promise** (reference what the title/thumbnail promised, or viewers feel tricked and bounce); (2) **promise a specific payoff** by ~0:15; (3) **open a loop / start delivering** so they commit. The steepest drop is seconds ~10–20, so land the payoff promise fast.
 
**The open-loop hook is NOT an announcement.** Create a knowledge gap + stakes, not a flat capability statement.
- ❌ "In this video I'll show you some AI tools for making videos." (announcement)
- ✅ "You only pay for **one** tool in this entire faceless-video stack — and it's not the one everyone's trying to sell you." (gap + stakes; the loop must actually close later)
**3-phase opening (0–30s):**
- **0–5s — pattern interrupt:** bold claim / unexpected visual / one-line question / *show the result first.* Your first words must beat whatever the viewer would skip to.
- **5–15s — specific payoff promise:** "by the end you'll have the exact 5-tool stack and know which one to pay for." Specific beats vague.
- **15–30s — commitment hook:** an information gap, proof of the result, or the start of the demo.
**Hook types that work:** curiosity gap, bold/contrarian claim ("most 'best AI tool' videos are wrong about this"), in-medias-res cold open, stakes/loss-framing ("do this wrong and you'll waste months"), visual hook, question hook, story hook, result-first ("here's the finished video — made in 20 minutes, now here's how").
 
**Hook formulas:**
- **PVSS (Proof → Value → Structure → Stakes)** for educational: why listen (a specific result/credential) → what they'll gain → the roadmap → what they risk by not following.
- **[specific number] + [specific claim] + [open question]:** "I tested 11 AI video tools. Only one didn't waste my money — and the reason surprised me."
- **Contrarian:** "Everyone says use [popular tool]. After testing it for a month, I stopped. Here's why."
**Hook mistakes that burn attention (cut these):** "Hey guys, welcome back," logo/intro bumper before content, "in this video we're going to talk about…," >10s of slow context, an early "like and subscribe" before you've earned it, clichés ("have you ever wondered…").

## 5. PACKAGING — TITLES, THUMBNAILS, A/B
 
*(Moved here from the channel skill — packaging is part of the creative artifact and ships with the script.)*
 
- **Titles:** 3–5 options. **Keyword-front-loaded for a new channel** (search is the realistic discovery path at low subs). Main idea in the first 3–5 words. Curiosity + specificity. Aligned to the thumbnail.
- **Thumbnails:** one focal point, high contrast, **readable at 10% size**, minimal text. **Don't duplicate the title** — the thumbnail is a *second* hook, not a repeat. On a tool-review, the tool's UI/logo + a verdict cue (✓/✗, "WORTH IT?") reads instantly.
- **Packaging must match the content.** A high-CTR thumbnail that over-promises tanks retention, and the algorithm rewards total watch time, not clicks. No clickbait the video doesn't pay off.
- **A/B testing — change ONE variable at a time.**
  - **Thumbnails:** ship 2–3 **distinct concepts** (different ideas, not three tweaks of one), run YouTube's **Test & Compare**, keep the winner.
  - **Titles:** run 2 variants; YouTube doesn't A/B titles natively, so swap the loser after ~7–14 days (or use vidIQ/TubeBuddy). Keep the keyword up front.
  - **Never change title and thumbnail in the same window** or you won't know which moved the metric.

## 6. RETENTION ARCHITECTURE (apply to every video)
 
- **Open-loop hook** (§4) — a gap, not an announcement; close it later.
- **Deliver value fast** (action formats) — give the punchline/overview early, then go deep.
- **Protect the mid-video drop zone (~55–65% of runtime).** Cumulative drop-off peaks here, often on the least exciting step. **Open that section with tension or stakes, never a flat transition.** ("Time to put the stack together" → "this is where most creators accidentally sabotage their whole video…")
- **Plant a reward at ~70%.** Hold back one genuinely strong tip/payoff, **tease it early** ("the big one — with the fix — is near the end"), and **deliver it around 70%** to pull viewers to the finish. Don't front-load every peak.
- **CTAs recur.** At least one soft mid-roll subscribe ask — ideally right after a payoff or verdict card (~70%) — plus the end CTA. Not only at the very end.
- **Pattern interrupt every ~30–45s; re-hook right before known drop-offs; no slow intro** (~33% drop in the first 30s).
**Re-hook / pattern-interrupt swipe file:** tonal shift · new visual or graphic · surprising stat · direct address ("watch this") · b-roll · screen-zoom or angle change · music change · a new open loop · a mini-cliffhanger ("but there's a catch — in a sec"). For 10+ min videos, a mid-video re-hook is mandatory, plus a clear one-sentence **bridge** between segments (bridges are a common failure point).
 
**What the retention graph teaches:** a cliff in the first 30s = hook failure (rework the hook); a gradual drift = pacing (add re-hooks/interrupts); a sudden dip at a fixed timestamp = a specific bad moment to cut.
 
**Session / binge (the algorithm rewards session time):** end by opening a loop the *next* video closes; organize content into playlists/series so one view becomes a session.

## 7. PERSUASION & MEMORY PSYCHOLOGY (how to USE each, in a line)
 
- **Curiosity gap (Loewenstein):** give just enough info to make the gap feel *closable* ("there are 3 reasons — the 3rd surprised me"). Tease, don't overwhelm (too-big gaps cause people to give up).
- **Zeigarnik / open loops:** open a question or story and explicitly hold it open ("I'll come back to that"). The driver is *tension/attention*, so use it as a cliffhanger.
- **Von Restorff (isolation effect):** make your single key takeaway **distinct** — a full-screen graphic, a beat of silence, a change in delivery — so it's the thing remembered. Use it to rescue an important mid-video point.
- **Primacy/recency:** strongest hook first, most important takeaway last; don't bury the key point in the middle unmarked.
- **Peak-end rule:** engineer one clear emotional/payoff **peak**, and **end strong** — a weak ending is penalized twice (in memory and in felt judgment).
- **Cognitive load:** one idea per sentence; chunk; never stack dense narration + heavy on-screen text + a complex visual in the same moment.
- **Dual coding / picture superiority (Paivio):** pair every key spoken point with a *reinforcing* visual — vital for a faceless channel. The visual should support, not compete with, the words.
- **Neural coupling (Hasson):** tell stories / mini-case-studies — narrative literally synchronizes the listener's attention, and clarity increases the coupling.
- **Cialdini's 7 (with channel uses):** **Authority** (specific test results/credentials early — ties to PVSS), **Social proof** ("the tool 300k creators switched to"), **Reciprocity** (give real value free, up front), **Commitment** (a small yes — "comment your current editing time"), **Liking** (warmth, "we creators"), **Scarcity/loss-framing** (honest only), **Unity** (shared identity — "us faceless creators").
- **Made to Stick — SUCCESs (Heath brothers):** **S**imple (find the core), **U**nexpected (break the pattern, then resolve), **C**oncrete (sensory/specific), **C**redible (vivid detail, real numbers), **E**motional (make them care about one person/use-case, not stats), **S**tories. Watch the **Curse of Knowledge** — experts default to abstraction.
- **Loss aversion / framing / anchoring:** frame stakes as losses ("stop wasting hours"); anchor with a reference number early; "imagine you already have X" (endowment) is persuasive. Keep all of it honest.

## 8. DELIVERY & PERFORMANCE — YOUR OWN VOICE
 
The narration is a **human performance over screen recordings.** Most delivery problems are written into the script *before* recording, so the script is also a delivery score.
 
### 8a. The 9 speaking habits (the foundation)
**Delivery habits (write these into the script):**
1. **Pause / use silence** — like paragraphs for the ear; without pauses, words blend into mush. A pause signals importance.
2. **Slow down** — control pace; slow deliberately when you want a point to land.
3. **Declarative statements** — cut rambling and hedge words ("maybe, I think, sort of, probably"); speak in clear, definitive lines.
**Vocal habits (perform these at the mic):**
4. **Warm up** — hum + lip trills before the take (not in the take).
5. **Breath support** — nose-breathe between lines; support from the diaphragm; place breaths at idea boundaries, never mid-sentence.
6. **Fuller volume / presence** — speak with vitality; listeners *feel* a confident voice. "Not just heard — felt." A small voice makes ideas seem small.
 
**Cognitive habits (write these into the script):**
7. **Finish your thoughts / close open loops** — don't topic-jump mid-idea; complete one thought before the next (fragmented speech sounds scattered even when the ideas are good).
8. **Use frameworks** — structure with the 3 C's: **Context → Concise (one idea) → Connect (why it matters to them).** (See the full library in §9.)
9. **Use analogies** — tie complex ideas to something familiar (see §10).
 
### 8b. The script-as-score markup (mark the VO before recording)
Mark these straight onto the VO column; read off the page:
 
| Mark | Meaning | Habit |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | short pause (a comma for the ear) | 1 |
| `//` | full stop — land it, let silence sit | 1 |
| `…` | trail off; don't rush the next line | 1 |
| `(breath)` | inhale here — at an idea boundary, never mid-sentence | 5 |
| `**word**` | punch it — weight + volume | 6 |
| `_word_` | slow down through this | 2 |
| `[↑]` / `[↓]` | lift energy / settle (quieter, intimate) | 6 / 2 |
| `\|` | beat break — a new idea starts; finish the last first | 7 |
 
Plus a **pronunciation pass**: rehearse tricky tool/proper names (ElevenLabs, DaVinci Resolve, CapCut) so they're said consistently; keep a short phonetic guide at the top of the script.
 
### 8c. Performance + recording
- **Own the ideas, don't memorize words** — internalize each beat so it sounds spontaneous; if reading off a teleprompter, match the scroll to your natural rhythm and lift your eyes between phrases.
- **Vocal variety:** vary pitch (higher = energy/urgency, lower = authority/calm), pace, and volume; emphasize the marked words.
- **"The mic eats energy"** — push energy ~20% past what feels natural; low energy is a top hook-killer. Match delivery energy to the importance of the line.
- **Record beat-by-beat**, not in one perfect take — reset breath + energy at each beat; it makes editing easier and keeps energy consistent.
- **Mic basics:** consistent distance, slightly off-axis to reduce plosives, monitor playback to catch monotone/rushing.
- **Editing supports delivery:** cut dead air and filler; **j-cuts** (next scene's audio starts under the current visual) smooth transitions; **b-roll/screen-recording masks cuts** so you can stitch takes; **but preserve deliberate dramatic pauses** — don't edit out the silence that makes a beat land.

## 9. FRAMEWORK LIBRARY (when to use, for long-form)
 
- **3 C's (Context → Concise → Connect):** the default per-segment structure — orient, one idea, why it matters to them.
- **PAS / AIDA / ABT / BAB:** see §3.
- **PASTOR (Problem → Amplify → Story/Solution → Transformation → Offer → Response):** longer persuasive/sales-style videos.
- **4 P's (Picture → Promise → Proof → Push):** demos and tool pitches.
- **Hero's Journey / Story Circle:** personal transformation arcs (Circle is the simpler 8-beat version).
- **Hook–Retain–Reward / Hook–Story–Offer / Curiosity Loop:** YouTube-native restatements of open-loop → payoff.
- **Feynman technique:** explain as if to a beginner → spot the gaps → simplify → add an analogy. A working analogy proves real understanding (and self-explaining makes it stick). Use for any AI concept the audience finds abstract.
- **StoryBrand / SB7 (Donald Miller):** **the viewer is the hero; you are the guide.** Hero wants something → has a problem → meets a guide (empathy + authority) → who gives a plan → calls them to action → helping them avoid failure → reach success. **Frame the viewer as the hero of your tutorial, yourself as the guide** — never the hero showing off.
- **Rule of three:** group points in threes ("three tools," "three steps") — memorable and rhythmic.
- **SUCCESs (Heath):** a checklist to pressure-test any idea for stickiness (§7).

## 10. ANALOGIES & EXPLANATION
 
**Principles of a great analogy:** (1) the base must be genuinely familiar to *your* audience; (2) map the correspondences explicitly (waiter = API, kitchen = server); (3) base and target should be superficially different but structurally similar; (4) **always say where the analogy breaks down** — incomplete analogies are the #1 cause of misconceptions; (5) follow the analogy with the formal rule to lock in correct understanding. Pair with **concreteness**, **show-don't-tell**, and live demonstrations.
 
**Analogy bank (ready to use):**
 
*AI / tech (most relevant here):*
- **API** = a restaurant waiter taking your order to the kitchen and bringing back the dish (menu = endpoints, kitchen = server, dish = response).
- **Training an AI model** = teaching a child to recognize a cat from many labeled photos (supervised = studying with an answer key; unsupervised = sorting toys by color with no instructions; reinforcement = training a dog with treats).
- **LLM / "it predicts the next word"** = the world's most well-read autocomplete.
- **RAM vs. storage** = your desk (active, fast, limited) vs. a filing cabinet (big, slower, long-term).
- **GPU for rendering** = a kitchen with 100 line cooks doing simple tasks at once vs. one master chef (CPU).
- **The cloud** = renting space in someone else's data center — literally "someone else's computer."
- **Encryption** = a locked box only the keyholder can open; **public/private key** = an open padlock anyone can snap shut, but only you hold the key that opens it.
- **Bandwidth** = the width of a pipe / number of highway lanes (capacity, not speed).
- **A render queue** = a single-file checkout line; more cores = more checkout lanes.
*Finance / value (for "is it worth it" framing):*
- **Compound interest / recurring affiliate income** = a snowball rolling downhill, picking up more snow — small and slow at first, then bigger and faster.
- **A one-time vs. recurring commission** = a single paycheck vs. a salary that keeps paying while the customer stays.
- **A subscription you forget to cancel** = a slow leak draining the tank.
*Science (for the occasional explainer):*
- **The immune system** = a layered national defense: skin = border walls; innate cells = police/paramedics; adaptive immunity = a trained standing army with memory.
- **A vaccine** = a wanted poster handed to your immune system so it recognizes the criminal before the real attack (it trains; it doesn't itself kill the virus).
- **Electricity** = water in pipes: voltage = pressure, current = flow, resistance = a narrow pipe (breaks down at AC — flag it).

## 11. THE DELIVERABLE TEMPLATE (every script ships as ONE production doc)
 
This format is battle-tested — reuse it. Blocks:
 
1. **Title options** — 3–5; primary recommended + one-line why; keyword-front-loaded for a new channel.
2. **Thumbnail concept(s) + A/B plan** — 2–3 *distinct* concepts and 2 title variants (per §5).
3. **The script — two-track:** **VO** (what you say, in your own voice) + **`[ ]` on-screen / production cues** (screen recording, b-roll, graphics — since faceless, the visuals carry the show). Mark the **hook**, **pattern interrupts / re-hooks**, the **~70% reward beat**, and **verdict cards** ("TOOL → worth it / skip"). Apply the **§8b markup** to the VO and put a **phonetic guide** for tricky tool names up top.
4. **Fact-check table** — every spec/price/claim with the **source**, the **date checked**, and a **"re-verify before publish"** flag. Include **platform-policy rows** (AI-disclosure only-if-visuals-warrant + inauthentic/material-variation + cleared music).
5. **Affiliate setup** — programs in **priority order** (recurring first), each with commission model + network + cookie window + payout minimum; **link placement** (description grouped by step + pinned-comment top 2–3); and the **disclosure text**.
6. **Pre-publish checklist** — the **"I tested" integrity** check; **re-verify all prices** (and policy wording); **Gate 2 compliance**; affiliate disclosure present; **pronunciation rehearsed**; runtime check.
7. **Decisions-log entry** to paste into the Notion log (decision, money/affiliate, new verified facts, next step).

## 12. PRE-WRITE & PRE-PUBLISH CHECKLIST
 
**Before writing:** Gate 1 passed · title + thumbnail concept chosen first · the one specific decision/payoff this video resolves (in the audience's words) · the facts gathered + dated.
 
**Script self-check:**
- [ ] Title + thumbnail + first line all promise the *same* thing; thumbnail readable at 10%.
- [ ] Hook is an open loop (not an announcement) and lands a specific payoff by ~0:15; no greeting/bumper/"in this video."
- [ ] Every transition passes the **but/therefore** test (no "and then").
- [ ] A re-hook/pattern interrupt every ~30–45s; a mid-video re-hook if 10+ min; one-sentence bridges between segments.
- [ ] Mid-video drop zone (~55–65%) opens on tension; a teased reward lands ~70%.
- [ ] Every abstract concept has an analogy + the formal rule + where it breaks down.
- [ ] Every key VO point has a reinforcing on-screen cue (faceless = visuals carry it).
- [ ] Honest verdicts present; "I tested" claims are backed; free-tool wins + no-commission picks stated.
- [ ] Draft cut ~30%; one idea per sentence; read aloud.
- [ ] VO marked for pauses/emphasis/breath (§8b); tricky names rehearsed.
- [ ] Ends on a peak + recurring CTA + a loop into the next video.
**Before publish:** re-verify prices + platform policy (day-of) · Gate 2 (disclosure only-if-warranted, material variation, cleared music) · affiliate links + disclosure live · dead air cut but dramatic pauses kept · update the Notion decisions log.

## 13. CAVEATS (stay honest)
 
- **Retention/title/CTR benchmarks are largely creator-reported, not official YouTube docs** (e.g. ">70% at 0:30", the "~15s cliff", "result-first ≈ high retention"). Directional only — validate against *your own* analytics and compare to similar-length videos in your niche, not absolutes.
- **The Zeigarnik *memory* effect is contested** (a 2025 meta-analysis found weak evidence). The reliable mechanism for video is *attention/tension* (cliffhangers), not improved recall.
- **Dual coding vs. distinctiveness** is debated academically; the practical advice (pair words with relevant visuals) holds regardless.
- **Some analogies mislead if unqualified** — "bandwidth = speed" (it's capacity), "DNA = blueprint" (scientists prefer "recipe"); always state the limit.
- **Copywriting frameworks aren't laws** — there's no proven universal fixed sequence of persuasion effects. Treat AIDA/PAS/etc. as scaffolds to adapt, not rules.
- **MrBeast-style tactics don't transfer wholesale** to talking-head/tutorial content — adapt the *principles* (front-loaded value, matched packaging, escalating payoff), not the spectacle.
- **Ethical persuasion:** scarcity/loss-framing and authority must reflect genuine value and real claims — manipulative or false framing erodes the exact trust that drives this channel's growth.
