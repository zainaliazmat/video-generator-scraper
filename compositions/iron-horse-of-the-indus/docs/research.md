# The Iron Horse of the Indus — Research Spine

Internal working doc for the ~9-minute (~540s) B&W parallax documentary on the railways of the Indus valley / colonial Punjab & Sindh → Pakistan Railways. This is the **verified chronological spine only** — not the script, not the storyboard.

Dates and names below were cross-checked against web sources (Wikipedia, FIBIwiki, Grace's Guide, IRFCA, Cambridge University Library Special Collections, museum collections) in June 2026. Where sources disagree or a claim is shaky, it is **flagged**. Treat every year as provisional until it is reconciled against the caption of the actual photograph that gets sourced for the shot.

Archive shorthand used in the beats:
- **BL/IOR** = British Library / India Office Records (company reports, official photos)
- **WC** = Wikimedia Commons (esp. category "Rail transport in Pakistan", "North Western Railway")
- **CUL** = Cambridge University Library Special Collections (Royal Commonwealth Society photo collections — strong on Sukkur/Lansdowne)
- **IRFCA** = Indian Railways Fan Club archive (scans of Railway Gazette, period engineering articles)
- **NYPL** = New York Public Library Digital Collections
- **IA** = Internet Archive (digitised railway reports, gazetteers, engineering journals)
- **NAM** = National Army Museum, London (strong on Khyber 1925)
- **NAP** = National Archives of Pakistan / Lahore Museum (verify rights case-by-case; often uncatalogued)

## The Spine — ~24 beats

### Act I — Steamers and the first iron (1850s–1870s)

**Beat 1 — 1850s · "The river before the rail"**
The Indus was the highway: country boats and, soon, the Indus Steam Flotilla moved freight and people up from Karachi toward Multan before any continuous track existed.
*Archive:* BL/IOR, IA (early gazetteers, steam-flotilla prints); WC for river/steamer engravings.
*Sanity check:* confirm any steamer image predates the rail line and isn't a later 1870s–80s flotilla photo mislabelled "early."

**Beat 2 — 1855 · "The Scinde Railway is chartered"**
The Scinde Railway Company was established by deed of settlement (March 1855) and incorporated by the Scinde Railway Act 1855 — the legal birth of railways in what is now Pakistan.
*Archive:* BL/IOR (company prospectus / Act), IA, Grace's Guide scans.
*Sanity check:* this is a paper milestone — use a document/map image, not a "first train" photo, which would be anachronistic.

**Beat 3 — 13 May 1861 · "Karachi to Kotri — first track open"**
The first public line, Karachi–Kotri (~108 miles / 174 km), opened — the first operating railway in the future Pakistan.
*Archive:* BL/IOR, WC ("Rail transport in Pakistan"), IA. Early Karachi terminus prints likely.
*Sanity check:* don't confuse the 1861 Karachi–Kotri opening with the much later (1896–98) Karachi Cantonment station building.

**Beat 4 — 1860 · "Lahore reaches Amritsar; the fortress station"**
The first train ran Lahore–Amritsar for public traffic (1860); Lahore Junction was built ~1859–60 as a literal fortress — turrets, loopholes, thick walls — a post-1857-Mutiny defensive design.
*Archive:* WC (Lahore station is heavily photographed), BL/IOR, NYPL.
*Sanity check:* Lahore station foundation 1859 / first train 1860 are well attested; the fortress towers seen today were added/strengthened over time — caption the photo by its actual date, not "1860."

**Beat 5 — 1870 · "Four companies become one — the Scinde, Punjab & Delhi Railway"**
Scinde Railway, Punjab Railway, the Indus Steam Flotilla and the Delhi Railway merged into the Scinde, Punjab & Delhi Railway (SP&DR) — Karachi to Delhi under one operator.
*Archive:* BL/IOR (SP&DR reports), Grace's Guide, IA.
*Sanity check:* the merger is **1870**, not 1861. 1861 = Karachi–Kotri opening only. Keep these distinct in any caption.

### Act II — Bridging the rivers (1870s–1890s)

**Beat 6 — 7 June 1878 · "The Empress Bridge over the Sutlej"**
Inaugurated 7 June 1878 (engineer William St John Galwey; designer Sir Alexander Rendel), between Bahawalpur and Adam Wahan — a defining early crossing of the five-rivers country.
*Archive:* BL/IOR, IA, WC.
*Sanity check:* it spans the **Sutlej**, not the Indus — easy to mislabel.

**Beat 7 — 24 May 1883 · "Crossing the Indus at Attock"**
The Attock Bridge (Attock Khurd–Khairabad Kund) opened to traffic 24 May 1883 — rail finally vaulted the Indus on the road north to Peshawar.
*Archive:* WC, BL/IOR, NYPL (Attock gorge is dramatic and well photographed).
*Sanity check:* there are later Attock bridges; verify the photo shows the original 1883 girder bridge, not a 20th-century replacement.

**Beat 8 — 1886 · "The North Western State Railway is forged"**
In January 1886 the SP&DR was merged with the Indus Valley State Railway, Punjab Northern State Railway, and sections of the Sind–Sagar / Sind–Pishin / Kandahar State railways to form the North Western State Railway (NWR) — the system this whole film orbits.
*Archive:* BL/IOR (NWR formation records), Grace's Guide, IA.
*Sanity check:* "State" railway = government-run; image a map/report/HQ rather than a single train.

**Beat 9 — 1886 · "Through the Bolan to Quetta"**
The first rail link to Quetta opened in 1886 via the Bolan Pass — strategic track toward the Afghan frontier.
*Archive:* WC, BL/IOR, IRFCA.
*Sanity check:* the **1886 Bolan** route is distinct from the later (1894/97) Mushkaf–Bolan all-weather line; don't merge them into one date.

**Beat 10 — 1887 · "The Chappar Rift and the Louise Margaret Bridge"**
The alternative Quetta route via Harnai–Khost–Chappar Rift opened 1887; the Louise Margaret Bridge (opened 1887, named for the Duchess of Connaught who opened it) crossed a gorge ~225 ft above the bed — among the most photogenic engineering on the system.
*Archive:* National Galleries of Scotland (Fred Bremner photos of Chappar Rift), CUL (Berridge Collection), WC, IRFCA.
*Sanity check:* the Chappar Rift line was destroyed by flood (1942) and lifted (1943); ensure imagery is captioned to the working era, not the ruin.

**Beat 11 — 5 Sept 1891 · "The Khojak Tunnel breaks through"**
Begun 14 April 1888; first engine through the ~3.9 km Khojak Tunnel under the Khwaja Amran range on 5 September 1891 — long the longest tunnel in the region (until Lowari, 2018).
*Archive:* IRFCA, WC, BL/IOR; the Khojak portal later appeared on Pakistani currency.
*Sanity check:* "1891" = first engine through; construction spanned 1888–91 — caption to the moment the photo shows.

**Beat 12 — 1889 · "Lansdowne Bridge spans the Indus at Sukkur"**
Completed 1889 (designed by Rendel); on completion the longest rigid cantilever/girder span in the world, joining Sukkur and Rohri across the Indus.
*Archive:* **CUL (Royal Commonwealth Society — outstanding 1885–1889 construction sequence)**, WC, BL/IOR, NYPL.
*Sanity check:* the parallel Ayub Bridge alongside it is **1962** — make sure the chosen photo shows only the single Lansdowne span if you want the 1889 read.

**Beat 13 — 1894 · "First through train into Quetta via Mushkaf–Bolan"**
After landslips repeatedly destroyed the Bolan alignment (1890s), the rebuilt all-weather Mushkaf–Bolan line carried the first "through" train into Quetta in 1894.
*Archive:* IRFCA, BL/IOR, WC.
*Sanity check:* dates here are messy in sources (orders 1890; through train 1894; "main route" framing later). Treat 1894 as the through-train year and flag if a sourced photo says otherwise.

### Act III — The system at its height (1900s–1930s)

**Beat 14 — c.1904–1908 · "The Mughalpura (Moghalpura) Workshops"**
The NWR's great workshops took their present Mughalpura site c.1904; major shops were shifted there from Lahore station c.1908 — manufacture, repair and overhaul of coaches, wagons and locomotives, an industrial heart of the line.
*Archive:* WC, BL/IOR, Lahore Museum / NAP (verify rights), IA.
*Sanity check:* the "1912" date sometimes cited is **not** confirmed; sources point to 1904 (site) / 1908 (shops moved). Caption to the documented date, and flag if your photo claims otherwise.

**Beat 15 — early 1900s · "Junctions and palaces of steam"**
The trunk stations — Karachi (Cantonment built 1896–98), Lahore, Rawalpindi, Multan, Peshawar — anchored the network; grand colonial-Mughal station architecture as civic statement.
*Archive:* WC (rich), NYPL, BL/IOR, postcards via IA.
*Sanity check:* station buildings were extended/rebuilt across decades; date the photo by the building shown, not the line's opening year.

**Beat 16 — c.1900s–1910s · "Workforce of the line"**
Anglo-Indian drivers and supervisors, and a vast Indian workforce of platelayers, gangmen, firemen and clerks, ran the NWR daily — the human machine behind the iron one.
*Archive:* BL/IOR, IA, WC; *India of the Past* / Anglo-Indian community memoirs for context (not images).
*Sanity check:* crowd/staff photos are often undated — use only ones with a credible year, and don't over-claim a specific date.

**Beat 17 — World War I, 1914–1918 · "The railway goes to war"**
The NWR moved troops, animals and matériel to the frontier and to the ports for overseas fronts; the strategic logic that built the frontier lines was now fully exercised.
*Archive:* IWM (Imperial War Museum) for WWI rail logistics, BL/IOR, IA.
*Sanity check:* distinguish genuine WWI-era NWR images from generic Indian-railway WWI stock; **imagery here is thinner** — see note below.

**Beat 18 — 3 Nov 1925 · "The Khyber Pass Railway opens"**
Jamrud–Landi Kotal opened 3 November 1925 (engineer Victor Bailey) — 34 tunnels, ~92 bridges/culverts, broad gauge through the Khyber, one of the boldest strategic railways ever built.
*Archive:* **NAM (the 1925 opening is well documented)**, IRFCA (Railway Gazette 1925), WC, BL/IOR.
*Sanity check:* 1925 = the Jamrud–Landi Kotal opening; earlier survey/abandoned-line dates exist — caption precisely.

**Beat 19 — 1920s–1930s · "Mail, frontier and the everyday timetable"**
At its peak the NWR ran prestige mail trains and dense frontier/branch traffic across Punjab, Sindh and Balochistan — the network at full maturity between the wars.
*Archive:* WC, IA (timetables, posters), BL/IOR.
*Sanity check:* "peak network" is a summary, not a dated event — pick photos with firm 1920s–30s captions; avoid passing off 1900s images as 1930s.

### Act IV — Partition and after (1947–1970s)

**Beat 20 — 1942–1943 · "The Chappar Rift is lost"** *(optional/transitional)*
A flash flood (night of 10 July 1942) swept away the scree bench under the Chappar Rift line; the Zardalu–Bostan section was closed and lifted in 1943 — the first great retreat of the network.
*Archive:* IRFCA, WC, National Galleries of Scotland (pre-loss images).
*Sanity check:* this is a niche beat — include only if the 24-beat count needs it; otherwise fold into Beat 10. Imagery is of the *intact* line, not the wash-out.

**Beat 21 — 14–15 Aug 1947 · "Partition cleaves the line"**
At independence the NWR was split: the bulk (in the new Pakistan) became the **Pakistan Western Railway** lineage; the Indian remnant went to the **Eastern Punjab Railway**. A single integrated system was severed at the new border (notably around Lahore–Amritsar–Wagah).
*Archive:* Partition Museum (Google Arts & Culture story "The Railways and Partition"), BL/IOR, WC.
*Sanity check:* the *operating* name remained "North Western Railway" in Pakistan for years (renamed 1961 — see Beat 23); 1947 is the **partition/split**, not the renaming. Keep those two apart.

**Beat 22 — Aug–Sept 1947 · "The refugee trains" — HANDLE SOBERLY**
**This beat is a human catastrophe, not spectacle.** An estimated ~700,000 refugees travelled by train between 15 Aug and 8 Sept 1947 alone; special trains carried Muslims toward Pakistan and Hindus/Sikhs toward India. Many were attacked; some arrived as "ghost trains" carrying only the dead (e.g. the Amritsar attack, 22 Sept 1947). Treat with restraint and dignity — implied loss, empty platforms, packed carriages, never gore, never a thrill beat.
*Archive:* Partition Museum / Google Arts & Culture, Science & Industry Museum (Manchester — Pakistan Railways loco SPS 3157 partition story), BL/IOR, press archives.
*Sanity check:* verify each image's date and side; partition photos are frequently mis-attributed. Do **not** caption a generic crowded-train photo as a specific massacre. When uncertain, choose the quieter, non-graphic frame.

**Beat 23 — 1 Feb 1961 · "North Western Railway becomes Pakistan Western Railway"**
On 1 February 1961 the Pakistan portion of the old NWR was formally renamed the Pakistan Western Railway (the East-Bengal portion of the Assam Bengal Railway had become the Pakistan Eastern Railway).
*Archive:* BL/IOR, WC, official Pakistan Railways materials.
*Sanity check:* this is the **renaming** (1961), distinct from the 1947 split and the 1974 merger/nationalisation. Three separate dates — keep them straight.

**Beat 24 — 1971/1974 · "One name: Pakistan Railways"**
After the loss of East Pakistan (1971) and the Pakistan Eastern Railway, the Western network was consolidated as **Pakistan Railways**; in May 1974 it was reorganised as an autonomous federal ministry — the modern national railway in its present name and form.
*Archive:* WC, Pakistan Railways / Ministry of Railways (Pakistan) materials, press archives, IA.
*Sanity check:* sources blur 1971 (de facto rename after the East's loss) vs 1974 (administrative reorganisation into a ministry). Present as a 1971→1974 transition, not a single clean date — **flagged below**.

## Contested / verify-before-final

- **NWR → Pakistan Western Railway renaming year.** One source implies 1947; the better-attested date is **1 Feb 1961**. 1947 = partition/split; 1961 = renaming. *Use 1961 for the rename; flag if a sourced caption says otherwise.*
- **"Pakistan Railways" date: 1971 vs 1974.** The post-East-Pakistan rename (1971) and the autonomous-ministry reorganisation (May 1974) are conflated in popular sources. Present as a 1971→1974 transition.
- **Mughalpura Workshops year.** "1912" is sometimes cited but **not confirmed**; sources support **1904** (present site) and **1908** (shops moved from Lahore station). Use 1904/1908.
- **Bolan vs Mushkaf–Bolan vs Chappar Rift to Quetta.** Three overlapping Quetta routes/dates (Bolan 1886; Chappar Rift/Harnai 1887; Mushkaf–Bolan through-train 1894). Easy to merge by mistake — keep them as separate beats with separate years.
- **Empress Bridge river.** It crosses the **Sutlej**, not the Indus. Frequently mislabelled.
- **Lansdowne vs Ayub Bridge at Sukkur.** Lansdowne = 1889; the adjacent Ayub Bridge = 1962. A photo with both is post-1962.
- **Lahore station "fortress" features.** Foundation 1859 / first train 1860 are solid, but the visible defensive towers were strengthened over time — caption by the photo's date.
- **Karachi Cantonment station 1896–98** vs the 1861 Karachi–Kotri opening — different things, ~35 years apart.
- **Refugee-train photos** are routinely mis-dated and mis-sided. Verify every one; default to restraint.

## Strong imagery is abundant for / thin for

**Abundant (visually safe — proceed confidently):**
- The great bridges: **Lansdowne/Sukkur** (CUL Royal Commonwealth Society has a full 1885–89 construction sequence), Attock, Empress.
- **Khyber Pass Railway, 1925** (NAM + Railway Gazette via IRFCA — strong, well-dated).
- **Chappar Rift / Louise Margaret Bridge** (Fred Bremner / National Galleries of Scotland; CUL Berridge).
- **Lahore Junction** and other trunk stations (heavily photographed on WC/NYPL).
- **Khojak Tunnel** portal (iconic; even on currency).

**Thinner (allow extra sourcing time; may need illustration, map, or document substitutes):**
- **WWI-era NWR logistics (Beat 17)** — generic Indian-railway WWI stock exists, but NWR-specific dated images are scarce.
- **Workforce / everyday-staff photos (Beat 16)** — exist but frequently undated; hard to caption precisely.
- **The 1855 chartering and 1870 SP&DR merger (Beats 2, 5)** — paper milestones; lean on documents/maps, not "first train" photos.
- **Refugee trains (Beat 22)** — images exist but are ethically and factually fraught; budget verification time and prefer quiet, non-graphic frames.
- **Indus Steam Flotilla (Beat 1)** — period prints/engravings more available than crisp photos; verify era.
